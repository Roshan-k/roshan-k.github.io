01 The problemEvidence

Six factors behind one purchase.

ChartVPS sells VPS and dedicated servers to traders. They had plenty of infrastructure information, but choosing the right plan took too much interpretation.

The challenge wasn’t making server plans easier to read. It was making the decision behind them easier to understand.

02 The complexityEvidence

Not a layout problem. A chain of translations.

Between a trader and a purchase sit six translations, each needing expertise the trader may not have. Every screen had to carry part of that chain without showing all of it.

Five tensions the design had to hold

  1. Technical depth↔SimplicityTraders who know hardware need all of it; most need to see past it.
  2. Performance evidence↔Cognitive loadBenchmarks prove fit, but every added figure slows the choice.
  3. User expertise↔GuidanceThe right plan depends on knowledge the product can’t assume, without talking down to traders who have it.
  4. User needs↔Business goalsPlans must sell; advice must stay honest enough to trust.
  5. Many entry points↔One journeyOffers bring people in from outside the pricing flow; they still have to reach a plan.

04.2 Key design decisionsDefine · Structure

Before designing screens, I designed the decision.

Four decisions shaped everything after. Each one is argued here, and shown in the solution.

Decision 01

Lead with performance, not hardware

Problem
Plan cards described the hardware, but not whether it fits a way of trading.
Decision
Make performance part of the purchase: benchmark bars under every price, latency in every specification.
Why
Traders don’t buy cores; they buy the confidence that their platform and strategy will run well.
Trade-off
Every metric on the card would bury the price. Each card carries two benchmark bars; the full breakdown moves to the benchmark panel, and the full specification stays, lower down.
See it in 05.1 Performance-led pricing ↓
Decision 02

Give every number a reason to matter

Problem
A benchmark score on its own means little to most traders.
Decision
Pair each headline figure with a percentile, a comparison and the trading task it affects.
Why
The number proves it, the comparison sizes it, the context makes it matter.
Trade-off
Context costs words. Only headline figures get the full treatment; the rest are percentile bars, readable at a glance.
See it in 05.2 Contextual performance ↓
Decision 03

Recommend, and show the cheaper option

Problem
Choosing a plan meant translating markets, style, platform and charts into hardware.
Decision
Ask five questions in trading terms, confirm the location, and return one plan with its reasons.
Why
A recommendation someone can check is one they can trust.
Trade-off
A finder can read as an upsell. Every result shows a cheaper plan beside it and a route to compare all plans — giving up the highest-priced answer to keep the advice credible.
See it in 05.3 Plan Finder ↓
Decision 04

Connect every entry point to one journey

Problem
Plans, the Plan Finder and offers were separate starting points, and offers had no clear place in the journey.
Decision
Every route — plans, the Plan Finder or an offer — arrives at the same plan detail, performance and purchase.
Why
A trader should reach the same decision whichever way they come in.
Trade-off
An offer never stands alone: it always resolves to plans and their performance, so promotions follow the plan structure rather than their own.
Status
Live on the homepage and in the Plan Finder; the Pricing and plan pages are next.
See it in 05.4 Promotions and 05.5 Connected purchasing journey ↓
04.3 Information hierarchy

Order, not removal.

Nothing was cut. Quick signals first, evidence next, full detail last — there when it’s wanted, never in the way.

05.1 Performance-led pricingDecision 01

Make performance part of plan selection.

Three series, each read in one order: purpose, price, performance, capability, specification, action.

Both early options showed the benchmark. Neither made it read as one: it looked like one more spec.

One card, four signals

Every benchmark figure comes from a test server set up with the plan’s specification and measured on a benchmark testing site. Bars show each score’s percentile against every computer in PassMark’s benchmark database, the same scale as the benchmark panel.

05.2 Contextual performanceDecision 02

Give every number a reason to matter.

Pick a series, then a plan. Every headline figure arrives as number, comparison, context and trading relevance.

05.3 Plan FinderDecision 03

A recommendation you can check.

Six steps from how someone trades to one plan, its reasons and a cheaper option.

On the result screen · 07

  1. One recommendationA plan and its price, not a shortlist to decode.
  2. Why this planEach reason quotes an answer back — three charts, scalping, US futures — so the recommendation can be checked.
  3. Spend lessA cheaper plan beside it, so the finder never reads as an upsell.
  4. Three ways forwardTake the plan, compare all plans, or start over.

06.1 OutcomeOutcome

What changed

Before“Which VPS should I buy?”

↓

After“Which infrastructure fits the way I trade?”

Before, traders had to compare every plan’s specs and work out fit themselves. Now the product starts from how they trade and tells them which plan fits, and why.

User

What became easier

Performance and plan fit became easier to evaluate, starting from how someone trades rather than from a specification sheet.

Product

What became connected

Pricing, performance, plan selection and promotional entry points became one purchasing experience.

Business

What it supported

Fewer support calls about which plan to choose, and better customer retention. The CEO pointed to better user growth.

System

What it established

One benchmark system across every series and plan: the same scores on the same percentile scale, so performance reads the same wherever it appears.

All four parts shipped and are live on chartvps.com: performance-led pricing, the benchmark panel, the Plan Finder and the Promotions page

06.2 Reflection

What’s next, and what stayed with me.

The redesign is live. Some of it is still unfinished, and some of it changed how I work.

Still open

The rollout isn’t finished. The homepage and Plan Finder are connected; on the Pricing page and individual plan pages, the cards don’t link to the benchmarks yet.

Test next

Where traders still drop off between choosing a plan and payment, and whether choosing a plan is getting faster.

What I took from it

Support feedback is design evidence: working closely with that team shaped this project. And traders buy performance when it’s explained as what it does for their trading, not as hardware.
