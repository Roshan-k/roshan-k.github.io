LabelCaster

From upload to payout.

A music distribution app where labels submit releases, agree on contracts and splits with their artists, and get paid. LabelCaster delivers each release to streaming services such as Spotify and Apple Music. This redesign and rebrand of the existing app makes the business side of music understandable to people who aren’t experts.

Role
Product designer · wireframes, visual design and user research
Platforms
iOS · Android
Status
Shipped as a private app

01 · Problem

Artists and labels use the same app, from opposite sides of a contract.

One sideArtists

Contribute to releases, review and sign agreements, and receive royalties.

Between themThe contract

Sent by one side, signed or rejected by the other.

Other sideLabels

Manage releases, agreements, splits and approvals. Join by invitation.

Labels submit releases to LabelCaster, which delivers them to streaming services.

The artist side uses the same screens in a light theme. Except where marked, the rest of this page shows the label side.

Signing a contract meant leaving the app.

The clearest of four problems in the old app. Three of them broke the path through a release; the fourth was that both sides saw one interface.

  1. Signing happened outside the appContracts were signed in an external tool, and the signed contract had to be kept in external storage.
  2. Navigation dead endsPeople got stuck on a screen with no way back.
  3. A complicated multi-track uploadEach song needed its own details, such as its name and ISRC code.
  4. One look for both sidesLabels and artists saw the same interface.

04 · The workflow

Five steps, from a sound file to a payout.

The redesign is organized around the work a release has to move through: adding music, agreeing terms, signing, completing publishing information, and tracking earnings.

  1. AAdd music

    Pick the release type, upload the files, name each track.

  2. BContracts & splits

    Choose a contract type, set every share, add costs if needed.

  3. CSign

    Read, then sign or reject, inside the app.

  4. DPublishing info

    Complete the composition details and publishing splits required for the release.

  5. EEarn & cash out

    Follow earnings, then request a payout.

05 · Key decisions

Five decisions carry the redesign.

01 · Step C

Bring signing into the app.

Problem
Contracts were signed in an external tool and stored outside the app.
Decision
Read, sign or reject, draw the signature and see it confirmed, without leaving the app.
Why
Users asked for it. Before, people had to leave the app and download the contract separately.
Trade-off
The team had to build and maintain an in-app signing flow on a signing API, instead of using a ready-made signing tool.
See it in the screens

02 · Step A

Name tracks during upload.

Problem
Uploading 4–7 tracks meant filling in every detail for each song.
Decision
Choose the release type first, upload several sound files at once, and name each one in place.
Why
Naming tracks during upload removes a separate upload step for each file.
Trade-off
Filling in details for each track was still there: users had to enter the data one track at a time.
See it in the screens

03 · Step B

Set the shares where the agreement is made.

Problem
Splits and cost-sharing are financial agreements that are easy to get wrong.
Decision
The label sets the splits, and chooses whether to add recuperation, while creating a contract or uploading a track or album. Each share sits beside its name.
Trade-off
The shares still need approval: they are sent to the artist, and fixed only after a reply.
See it in the screens

04 · Every step

Explain the business where it happens.

Problem
ISRC codes, publishing royalties, IPI numbers, splits and recuperation. Most artists and many small labels aren’t experts in them.
Decision
Explain each concept on the screen where it’s needed, in plain language.
Why
The payout fee, for example, is shown before requesting, so people know the cost before they commit.
Trade-off
More text on screens that were already dense.

05 · Every screen

A way back from every screen.

Problem
People got stuck on a screen with no way back. Screens were overcrowded.
Decision
Every part of the app went into one main navigation menu, and every screen got a back button.
Why
“Sometime I stuck navigation around the screens, unable to go back or explore any feature.” Survey response

08 · Outcome

The redesign went live as a private app.

Access was given to approved accounts only. The app isn’t listed publicly in the stores.

The redesign brought the core release workflow into one product experience: adding music, agreeing splits, signing, publishing information and getting paid. It also set up one visual and interaction system for both sides of the label–artist relationship.

What shipped

  • Signing inside the app
  • A way back from every screen
  • Tracks named during upload
  • Shares beside names, recuperation explained first
  • The payout fee before the request
  • A new look and a design system, with a light theme for artists

Not yet known

No usage results are shown here. Whether these changes worked is what the questions below would test.

09 · What remains open

Four questions still open.

  1. Does naming tracks during bulk upload make adding an album faster?
  2. Do people understand recuperation before they add expenses?
  3. Does showing the payout fee first reduce surprise at cash-out?
  4. How often do splits fail to add up to 100% on the first try?

One thing I’d change now

The small text is too faint.

Secondary text is too faint on both themes, and worst on the artist side: sage-green secondary text on white and pale green, at about 1.9:1. On the label side, the dim-dark token reaches 2.8:1. Both are below the 4.5:1 WCAG minimum for body text.
